Oma'S East German Potato Salad

Let's bring back this East German Potato Salad, a DDR classic that will make you wonder why you never had it before. Yes, this is "Kartoffelsalat mit Fleischsalat," potato salad with meat salad, and it's meaty, creamy, and aromatic, a refreshing BBQ side that becomes the star of the party.

It's not my Oma's salad, it's the salad of Oma Karin from Dresden, the grandmother of my friend Doro. Doro is my number one source for East German recipes, and thanks to her we have Quarkkeulchen and Blueberry Dumplings on the blog too. Let's jump into this GDR potato salad recipe.

What Makes East German Potato Salad Different

Germany doesn't have one potato salad, it has a whole map of them according to it's regions. East German potato salad is creamy rather than tangy, built around Fleischsalat (a mayo-based deli meat salad) instead of vinegar or bacon. It's meant to be served cold, which sets it apart from most of what shows up when Americans search "German potato salad."

If you've tried my Warm Bavarian-Style Potato Salad with Bacon, you already know that version leans savory and warm, built on a bacon-vinegar dressing you pour over the potatoes while everything's still hot. Head further south and you'll find my Swabian Potato Salad, which skips mayo entirely. Up north, my Northern German Potato Salad is closer in spirit to what Americans expect, made with mayonnaise (affiliate link) and hard-boiled eggs, though it still leans milder and less sweet than the classic US picnic version.

East German potato salad takes its own path entirely. No bacon, no broth, no vinegar dressing, just cold potatoes tossed with Fleischsalat, a touch of mustard (affiliate link), and a splash of water to keep everything silky. It's the potato salad of Saxony and the wider former East Germany, and it's every bit as classic as its warmer, tangier, or egg-flecked cousins from other corners of the country.

Ingredients You'll Need

This is a short ingredient list, and every piece pulls its weight. You'll need potatoes (a waxy variety like Yukon Gold holds its shape best), Fleischsalat (the German deli meat salad that gives this dish its signature creaminess, more on that below if you can't find it locally), half an English cucumber sliced paper thin, a few spring onions, and a handful of fresh parsley.

For the dressing, you'll whisk together mayonnaise (affiliate link), a spoonful of Dijon or German mustard (affiliate link), salt and black pepper, and a splash of lukewarm water stirred in gradually, Doro's trick for keeping the whole salad silky instead of heavy.

How to Make East German Potato Salad

  1. Boil the potatoes in salted water until fork-tender, then peel while still warm and cut into bite-sized pieces.
  2. Slice the cucumber paper thin and toss it with the potatoes, Fleischsalat, spring onions, and parsley.
  3. Whisk up the mayo-mustard (affiliate link) dressing and pour it over the salad, folding gently to combine.
  4. Stir in the lukewarm water a little at a time until the salad turns silky and creamy.
  5. Chill for a couple of hours so the flavors settle.

Variations

Like most East German family recipes, this one has plenty of room to adapt. A few traditional twists worth trying:

  • Apple: Dice a tart green apple and fold it in for a touch of sweetness against the creamy dressing, a common addition in many Saxon households.
  • Hard-boiled eggs: Chop up two or three and mix them in, or slice them and lay them on top for a classic buffet-style presentation.
  • Extra meaty: Increase the Fleischsalat to 350-400g if you want a heartier, more filling salad. Since Fleischsalat already carries its own mayo, cut back slightly on the added mayonnaise (affiliate link) to keep the texture balanced.
  • Crispy bacon: Fry up some diced bacon (Bauchspeck) until golden and stir it in for extra richness and a smoky note.
  • Gherkins or pickle brine: A few chopped gherkins, or a spoonful of their brine stirred into the dressing, brings a nice tangy contrast to the creaminess.
  • Radishes: Thinly sliced, for a peppery crunch and a fresh pop of color.
  • Chives instead of parsley: Just as traditional, and a little milder in flavor.

How to Serve

This salad is a natural at any backyard barbecue, serve it alongside Schwenkbraten or your favorite grilled sausages for a proper German-style cookout. It also has a strong holiday tradition of its own: in East Germany, this style of potato salad is a Christmas Eve classic, served cold alongside warm Bockwurst or Wiener sausages, a pairing that's stayed a fixture on tables since DDR times.

Storage and Make-Ahead Tips

This salad actually gets better after a couple of hours in the fridge, so it's a great make-ahead option for a party or barbecue. Store it covered in the fridge for up to 3 days. As it sits, the potatoes soak up the dressing and the salad can look a little dry, that's completely normal, just stir in a splash of lukewarm water to bring the creaminess back before serving.

I wouldn't recommend freezing it, since both the potatoes and the mayo-based dressing lose their texture once thawed.

Oma's East German Potato Salad

A creamy East German potato salad made with Fleischsalat (German deli meat salad), crisp cucumber, and a light mustard (affiliate link)-mayo dressing loosened with a splash of water instead of piling on more mayo. This DDR classic comes straight from Oma Karin of Dresden.
Print Recipe Pin Recipe
Prep Time 20 minutes mins
Cook Time 20 minutes mins
Total Time 40 minutes mins
Course Main Course
Cuisine German
Servings 4 people

Ingredients
 

  • 1 kg (2 lb) potatoes waxy variety like Yukon Gold
  • 1 teaspoon salt for the boiling water
  • ½ English cucumber sliced paper thin
  • 250 grams (½ lb) Fleischsalat German deli meat salad, see my recipe on how to make it from scratch
  • 3 spring onions thinly sliced
  • 3 tablespoons fresh parsley chopped
  • 3 tablespoons mayonnaise
  • 1 teaspoons Dijon or German mustard (affiliate link)
  • 1 teaspoons salt
  • ¼ teaspoons black pepper
  • ½ cups lukewarm water plus more as needed
Prevent your screen from going dark

Instructions
 

  • To start, scrub the potatoes under running water and place them whole, skin on, in a large pot. Cover the potatoes with cold water, add the salt, and bring to a boil. Cook until fork-tender for approximatley 20 minutes.
  • Drain the potatoes and let them cool just enough to handle, then peel while still warm, the skins slip off easily this way. Once the potatoes are cooled slice into bite-sized pieces.
  • While the potatoes cool, grab a mandoline or a very sharp knife and slice the cucumber (skin on) paper thin.
  • In a large bowl, carefully toss together the potatoes, cucumber, Fleischsalat, spring onions, and parsley.
  • In a separate small bowl, whisk together the mayonnaise (affiliate link), mustard (affiliate link), salt, and pepper until smooth.
  • Pour the dressing over the salad and gently fold everything together, being careful not to mash the potatoes.
  • Now pour in the lukewarm water, a little at a time, stirring alternatly until the salad turns smooth and creamy.
  • Cover and let it sit at room temperature or in the fridge for at least 2 hours so the flavors have time to come together.
  • Give it a taste, adjust seasoning if necessary, and stirr again before serving, and if it's looking a little dry, just stir in another splash of lukewarm water to bring the dressing back to life.
