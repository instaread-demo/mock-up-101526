Iced Butterfly Pea Tea

Iced Butterfly Pea Tea is a limeade drink that’s like a magic show that’ll make your friends say, “Wow! How did it just change color?!”

It starts out a deep blue, then after you add some fresh lime juice it becomes a vibrant violet purple! Add a little sugar, plenty of ice, and you’ve got a sweet-tart, refreshing drink that feels like a party in a glass!

What Is Butterfly Pea Tea?

Butterfly pea tea is made by steeping the deep blue flowers of the butterfly pea vine in hot water and adding lime juice and sugar to taste. The flower flavor is very mild and a little earthy, so their real superpower is their anthocyanins, natural pigments that give the petals their beautiful blue color plus antioxidants!

And where did the English name come from? The flower looks a little like a butterfly, and the vine belongs to the pea family, so butterfly pea! The plant grows throughout tropical areas of Southeast Asia, including Thailand, where you might see it climbing in a home garden or growing in huge cultivated fields.

In Thailand, the flower is called dok anchan (ดอกอัญชัน), and a drink made with it is often called nam anchan (น้ำอัญชัน). Dok means “flower,” and nam means “water” or “drink.”

Butterfly pea flowers have been used in Southeast Asian drinks and cooking for generations. In Thailand, cooks use the blue flower extract to color rice and desserts, including flower-shaped dumplings called cho muang. The fresh flowers can be eaten, too, or dipped in batter and fried. A tiny flower, doing all kinds of fun, powerful things in the kitchen! Love it!

Why You’ll Love This Butterfly Pea Tea Recipe

  • It’s sweet, citrusy, and so refreshing over ice
  • The blue-to-purple color change is super fun kitchen magic
  • You only need a few simple ingredients
  • You can make it still or fizzy with sparkling water

Iced Butterfly Pea Tea Ingredients You’ll Need

  • ½ cup dried butterfly pea flowers – These give the tea its deep blue color. Make sure you’re using dried flowers, not tea leaves.
  • 5 cups water – This is what the flowers steep in to make your blue tea.
  • ¾ cup lime juice, about 6 limes – Fresh lime juice brings all the tangy, citrusy flavor and creates that fun color change!
  • ¾ to 1 cup sugar – Start with ¾ cup, then sweeten it to your taste.
  • 5 cups sparkling water or cold water – Choose sparkling for a little fizz or regular water for a classic pitcher of iced tea.
  • Ice, for serving – Because this drink is made for a tall, cold glass!

How to Make Iced Butterfly Pea Tea

Step 1: Steep the butterfly pea flowers

Bring 5 cups water to a boil. Turn off the heat, stir in ½ cup dried butterfly pea flowers, and let them steep for 4 minutes. The water will turn a deep blue!

Step 2: Strain and sweeten

Strain out the flowers, then stir ¾ cup -1 cup sugar into the warm tea until it dissolves. Taste it! If you like your drinks on the sweeter side, add more sugar. Remember it will go over ice, so the flavor will dilute it some, so you might want more sugar than you think.

Step 3: Add the water and lime

Stir in 5 cups cold water. Now pour in ¾ cup lime juice and watch the blue turn purple, or wait until your guests come to do it, or add lime juice to each individual glass and let them mix it up!

If you’re using sparkling water, stir it in gently right before serving so you don’t lose all those fun bubbles.

Step 4: Pour over ice and enjoy!

Fill glasses with ice, pour in your colorful tea, and get ready for everyone to ask how you made it!

Ways to Customize Butterfly Pea Tea

  • Honey– Honey pairs well with the mellow flower flavor.
  • Orange juice – Add a splash for a sweeter citrus flavor. The acidity may change the color too!
  • Lemongrass – Steep a bruised stalk with the flowers for a light, fragrant flavor.
  • Milk – For a creamy version, pour the tea over ice and add milk or sweetened condensed milk. The color will turn more pastel, and taste richer.
  • Cocktails – Use chilled butterfly pea tea as a colorful mixer with gin, vodka, or rum.
  • Ginger – Steep a few slices with the flowers for a spicy note that’s especially cozy served warm.

Behind the Iced Butterfly Pea Tea Recipe

It’s SO hot in Thailand that Thai iced drinks are like little mini oasis from the heat for Thais and visitors. Drink stands line the streets, each with a long menu of colorful drinks.

Every trip, I spot something new, or find an old favorite I somehow missed because there are just so many Thai drinks to try!

Butterfly pea tea, or nam anchan, was one I didn’t discover for a while. Then I spotted it on the menu at O’s Thai Place in West Jefferson, one of our favorite local Thai restaurants. My Thai Hubby got excited, especially because back then, a drink like iced butterfly pea tea wasn’t something you saw every day in our little mountain town.

When the drink arrived, the lime juice was poured right in. We stirred it together and watched the blue tea turn violet. I loved how one squeeze of lime made the drink feel so fun!

So, of course, the next time we were in Thailand, I ordered it from the first drink vendor I saw. Then I picked up dried butterfly pea flowers at the Asian market so I could make it for friends back home.

What I love about it is that it’s caffeine free, unlike Thai Milk Tea or Thai Green Tea, so when I have friends over for dinner or I’m hosting a Thai cooking class, I can make it and everyone can drink it without worrying if it will keep them up at night!

Iced Butterfly Pea Tea Frequently Asked Questions

Does butterfly pea tea have caffeine?

Nope! Butterfly pea flowers are naturally caffeine-free, so this is a fun drink to sip any time of day.

What does butterfly pea tea taste like?

The tea has a mild, earthy flavor. Lime juice adds a tangy citrus punch, and sugar balances it with sweetness. Think refreshing, extra pretty limeade!

Why does butterfly pea tea change color with lime juice?

Butterfly pea flowers contain natural pigments called anthocyanin that give the tea its deep blue color, and are sensitive to acidity. When you add lime juice, its acid changes the tea’s pH, which changes how the pigments interact with light. That’s why the blue tea shifts toward purple, pink, or violet, depending on how much lime you add! So fun!

Can I make this drink ahead of time?

Yes! Make the tea, strain it, and let it cool. Refrigerate it until you’re ready to serve. If you’re using sparkling water, add that right before pouring so it stays bubbly.

Can I make it less sweet?

Of course! Start with less sugar, taste, and add more only if you want to. You can also add extra water or lime juice to balance it to your liking.
