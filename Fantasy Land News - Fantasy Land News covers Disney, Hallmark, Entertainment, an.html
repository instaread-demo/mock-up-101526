Disney and Character.AI Explained: From Cease-and-Desist to $20 Million Bankruptcy Bid

October 13, 2026

Written by Greg Gately

Sponsored by
0:00 / 3:59
Instaread

Disney is the lead bidder for part of Character.AI, the AI chatbot company that filed for bankruptcy on October 12. If the deal goes through, Disney would pay $20 million for a license to some of Character.AI’s technology. It would not be buying the app itself, and nothing changes for Disney fans today.

Character.AI is an app where people chat with AI-powered characters, many of them created by other users. Not long ago it was one of the hottest AI start-ups around. In 2024, Google paid a reported $2.7 billion to license its technology and bring its founders back to Google. Since then, the company has struggled with the high cost of running its AI and with lawsuits over the safety of younger users, including one filed by the state of Pennsylvania in May.

What Disney Is Actually Buying

Chapter 11 is a type of bankruptcy that lets a company keep operating while a court oversees a sale or restructuring. Disney is what’s called the stalking horse bidder. Its $20 million offer is the opening bid, and other companies can still top it at auction.

Character.AI is putting all or substantially all of its assets up for sale, and it says it hopes a buyer will keep the platform running. Disney’s opening bid is much narrower. It covers a non-exclusive, permanent license to some of Character.AI’s intellectual property. Non-exclusive means Character.AI can still license the same technology to someone else. Think of it as Disney getting its own copy of the keys, not buying the house. The company expects the sale to wrap up by the end of November or early December, and if no buyer steps up to keep the platform going, it will be wound down.

The sale is expected to close in late November or early December. Character.AI says the app will keep running during the auction, but it could shut down if no buyer agrees to operate it.

This has been building for about a year. In September 2025, Disney sent Character.AI a cease-and-desist letter over chatbots using Disney characters without permission, and Character.AI took them down. Three months later, Disney made its big AI bet, investing $1 billion in OpenAI and licensing more than 200 characters to Sora. Then in March, OpenAI shut Sora down before the Disney fan videos ever launched.

In September, Disney named Karandeep Anand, Character.AI’s CEO, as its first chief technology officer, reporting to Disney CEO Josh D’Amaro. He started October 2, and several other Character.AI employees have joined Disney too. Ten days later, Character.AI filed for bankruptcy with Disney first in line.

For Disney fans, nothing changes right now. No Mickey Mouse chatbot has been announced, and Disney has not said how it would use the technology. Disney is already testing AI in smaller ways, like the AI-powered search tools in beta on Disney+ and ESPN, and it told investors this summer that it uses AI simulation tools to help design new attractions, including for its Abu Dhabi park. Disney reports its full-year results on November 17, right around when this sale should close, so listen for any mention of AI on that call.

Our take is that when Sora shut down in March, we said the Mouse would find another door. This looks like that door, and it costs $20 million instead of $1 billion. In about a year, Disney went from sending Character.AI a legal warning to hiring its CEO to bidding on its technology. The part to watch is how Disney uses it. Fans will welcome AI that makes a Disney app easier to use a lot faster than AI that tries to stand in for the characters themselves.
