    When Amzchef reached out asking me to try their latest all-in-one espresso machine, I didn't hesitiate. I was already using their coffee maker, grinder, and milk frother at the time. However, the idea of one small appliance combining all three and saving precious kitchen counter space won me over!

    Now let's see if the individual features of this device hold up to their tasks. My husband and I are coffee enthusiasts. With our 2-3 daily cups each we quickly learned about and tested out all the different functions of this small appliance.

    Video

    Built-In Coffee Grinder

    First up the coffee grinder. There is no doubt that freshly ground coffee beans always produce a superior cup of coffee. Having an espresso machine that holds a decent amount of coffee beans and grinds them directly into the portafilter is peak convenience.

    Unlike when I had a separate grinder, I am not tempted to grind a bigger batch and just scoop it into the portafilter. Every shot of espresso is made with just-ground beans.

    The grinder is a precision burr grinder with 44-settings producing anywhere from super fine to extra course grinds. We've dialed in our perfect setting for this espresso machine at 8. Anything finer and the coffee machine just doesn't produce enough pressure for a proper extraction.

    The portafilter hooks neatly under the grinder to fill right into it. The included dosing ring works like a funnel and keeps the coffee grinds from overflowing. The heavy-duty stainless steel tamper with wooden handle makes compacting the grinds very easy.

    You can set the timer to grind anywhere from 1 to 25 seconds in .5-second intervals. For a double shot we've dialed in the ideal time at 4 seconds for us. If you want to grind some finer or coarser beans to use for a different brewing method like a French press, pour-over, or moka pot, you can fit a small bowl or cup under the grinder.

    Brewing Espresso: Hot or Cold

    To start brewing espresso, you'll first want to fill up the large capacity water tank with filtered water. It holds up to 60-ounces (~1.8L) making refills necessary less frequently.

    Hot

    This machine can brew hot coffee at 4 different temperatures ranging from 88-94°C (190-201°F). We use medium-dark roasted beans and always go with the highest temperature setting. Without fail it always produces a beautifully rich espresso with crema layer on top.

    On the LED display you can choose between single shot and double shot. Be sure to use the corresponding portafilter basket. Furthermore, the brew time for each button can still be adjusted to fully customize your shot4 (10-60 seconds).

    Cold

    The second option is to make cold coffee. This unique feature really sets this model apart from any others. The manual calls this the cold brew function, although traditional cold brew relies on time to achieve a very mellow coffee extraction without heat.

    Here the cold espresso is pulled with pressure and a lower, though not exactly cold temperature (98°F/37°C). The recommendation is to brew it into a cup with ice cubes to rapidly chill the cold brew even more.

    Admittedly, I was skeptical about this cold espresso extraction. Judging by sight the coffee shot looks pale and watery, so I didn't expect it to have much flavor. But much to my surprise, the cold brew has a pleasant and well balanced taste. Similar to a regular cold brew, the coffee flavor is milder and less acidic.

    It's perfect enjoyed just over ice as an iced americano, or added to ice and cold milk for an iced latte.

    Steam Wand

    The third elemental function of this appliance is the 8mm (0.3") stainless steel steam wand powered by a 1350W heating system. It can be used by simply turning the dial to add hot water to espresso for making an americano.

    Or press the steam button first to preheat, then steam milk to create silky foam for lattes, cappuccinos, macchiatos and more café quality drinks. The steam wand - while not completely automated - is very beginner-friendly to figure out.

    I am by no means a latte art expert. So for my daily latte I've settled on steaming a ½ cup of milk in my favorite mug, then I place it under the portafilter and brew the espresso shot straight into the milk foam. This has resulted in a few accidental latte art images and maybe eventually I'll learn the proper way too.

    Additionally, the steam wand also works well to make hot cocoa. Just add the hot chocolate mix and milk to a mug and steam and swirl until combined and heated through. This also works with other instant hot drink mixes (chai, golden milk, mushroom coffee, etc.)

    Pros And Cons Of The Amzchef Espresso Machine

    Pros

    • Built-in grinder - Grinding and espresso brewing combined in one machine, saving counter space and reducing the need for separate equipment
    • Temperature control - Adjustable temperature range from 190°F to 201°F, to tailor brewing for different beans and roasts
    • Powerful steam wand - The 8 mm stainless-steel wand creates microfoam for lattes, cappuccinos, and other milk-based drinks
    • 60 oz water tank - Provides enough capacity for many servings before needing a refill.
    • Cold brew mode - Dedicated setting for preparing cold coffee
    • 3-year warranty - Extended warranty if purchased from Amzchef website

    Cons

    • Large countertop footprint - it requires a reasonable amount of counter space (12.5" deep × 10.82" wide × 13.38" high)
    • Learning curve - Having 44 grind settings and adjustable temperature gives you control, but beginners may need some experimentation to find the right combination
    • Manual milk steaming - The steam wand provides control, but creating consistent micro foam requires practice. Not ideal for users looking for fully automated brewing
