TRIBUTES have been paid to a much-loved former Dorset Echo journalist who has died suddenly at the age of 58.

Ruth Meech, a former reporter and features writer at the Echo for 25 years, passed away whilst on holiday in Spain.

The Dorchester resident leaves behind her husband Jon and daughter Fran.

Ruth is remembered by former colleagues as someone whose “smile lit up the room,” and during her time at the Echo, which she joined as a trainee in 1989, she covered a huge range of subjects.

As a news reporter, she also oversaw health and ‘Teen Scene’ columns before moving to the paper’s features department.

On a weekly basis readers were enthralled by her column about bringing up her daughter and her side-splitting diary charting her attempts to get into shape with the help of a personal trainer.

Ruth would throw herself into every challenge, whether it was trying out cookery school, flying high above Dorset during parasailing lessons, taking golf lessons from Weymouth Golf Club’s professional or channelling her inner Simon Cowell as a judge helping Weymouth Pavilion with its Search for a Star for its Christmas pantomime.

The talented journalist had a way with words that made many a young trainee reporter want to write like her, while her warmth made them want to be her friend too.

Her copy, particularly the numerous articles chronicling her umpteen escapades, was compelling, self-effacing and downright hilarious.

Reviewing a Michelin-starred restaurant in Dorset, she once wrote: “Words have yet to be invented to do the dish justice, but if I have to die by drowning, I want to go in a vat of this soup.”

Small in stature, with her trademark shock of unruly curls, Ruth would often be the biggest and most vibrant personality in the newsroom.

She possessed the sharpest sense of humour but, alongside it, the kindest of natures, gently guiding many a young reporter finding their feet.

She left a gaping hole when she departed the Echo in 2014 to take up a role at Amateur Gardening magazine, conspiratorially whispering: “I’ve never gardened before!”

Her love of the natural world and writing talent saw her excel as a gardening writer. Her colleagues at the magazine described her as “a fantastic gardening journalist, plantswoman and joyful person who spread little seeds of care and consideration wherever she went.”

Outside work, Ruth always found time to give something back.

She volunteered as a Brownies leader and later became a valued member of Dorchester Drama, Women of Dorset’s Unearthed project, and a marshal at the weekly parkrun in Poundbury, where she was never short of words of encouragement for those bringing up the rear.

And she never stopped living life to the full with Fran and Jon.

The free-spirited trio were keen festivalgoers at Dorset’s End of the Road Festival, while Ruth and ‘Mr Hayes’, as she affectionately called her husband, would regularly visit Fran in London, never letting the distance get in the way of their closeness.

A service celebrating Ruth’s life will take place on Wednesday, October 21 at 2pm at The Barn at Higher Ground Meadow, followed by the interment of her ashes in The Orchard.

Those attending are invited to wear an item of colourful clothing.

A wake will follow at The Winyard’s Gap Inn from 3.30pm to 5.30pm.
